About the Assembly

The unicameral legislature of Gombe State: its mandate, its history and the way a bill becomes law.

Who we are

The legislative arm of the Government of Gombe State

The Gombe State House of Assembly is a unicameral legislature of twenty-four members elected from twenty-four state constituencies drawn across the eleven local government areas of the state. Local government areas with larger populations — Akko and Yamaltu/Deba — return three members each so that representation remains broadly equal.

Members are elected for a term of four years concurrent with that of federal legislators. The House sits three times a week in the Assembly Complex in Gombe, the state capital, and its committees work throughout the legislative year, including during adjournments.

The fundamental functions of the Assembly are to enact new laws, amend or repeal existing ones, appropriate public funds and exercise oversight of the executive. The House also screens and confirms nominees presented by the Governor for executive and statutory offices, and receives petitions from members of the public.

Vision

A responsive, transparent and people-centred legislature whose laws measurably improve the lives of the people of Gombe State.

Mission

To make good laws, appropriate public resources prudently, scrutinise the work of the executive rigorously, and keep an open door to every citizen of the state.

Core values

  • Equity — every constituency, party and community is heard on the floor.
  • Accountability — public money is tracked from appropriation to delivery.
  • Openness — sittings are public, hearings are advertised, records are available.
  • Diligence — bills are examined clause by clause, with evidence from stakeholders.
  • Independence — a separate arm of government working cooperatively, not subordinately.

Legislative autonomy. Following the constitutional amendment granting financial autonomy to state legislatures, the House manages its own budget through the Assembly Service Commission, strengthening its independence in carrying out oversight.

The legislative process

How a bill becomes law in Gombe State

A bill may be an executive bill transmitted by the Governor or a private member's bill sponsored by a member of the House. Either way it passes through seven stages.

First reading

The bill is formally introduced and its long title read by the Clerk. There is no debate at this stage.

Second reading

Members debate the general principles and purpose of the bill. If supported on a vote, the bill is committed to a standing or ad-hoc committee.

Committee stage & public hearing

The committee examines the bill clause by clause, invites written memoranda and holds a public hearing with stakeholders, ministries and affected communities.

Report stage

The committee lays its report before the House. Members consider the recommended amendments and may add, alter or delete clauses.

Third reading

The bill as amended is read a third time and put to the vote. On passage it is clean-copied and endorsed by the Clerk and the Speaker.

Transmission for assent

The bill is transmitted to the Governor, who has thirty days to assent or withhold assent and communicate reasons.

Assent, veto override and commencement

On assent the bill becomes law. Where assent is withheld, the House may pass the bill again by a two-thirds majority, after which it becomes law without assent. Laws are then gazetted.

You can take part. At committee stage the House invites written memoranda and holds public hearings. Watch the News page for notices, or write to the Office of the Clerk to be placed on the stakeholder list for a particular bill.

History

Milestones of the House

1996

Creation of Gombe State

Gombe State is carved out of Bauchi State on 1 October 1996.

6 February 1999

First parliament

The first House of Assembly of the new state is constituted following the return to civil rule.

1999 – 2019

Six Assemblies

Successive Assemblies legislate on local government administration, revenue, education, health and chieftaincy affairs.

June 2019

Sixth Assembly inaugurated

Twenty-four members are sworn in; eighteen serve for the first time.

November 2020

Change of leadership

Hon. Muhammad Abubakar Luggerewo, member for Akko Central and then Chairman of the Finance Committee, is elected Speaker.

June 2023

Seventh Assembly inaugurated

The Speaker is re-elected to lead the Seventh Assembly, with the APC holding twenty seats and the PDP four.

November 2024

New complex begins

Groundbreaking for a new Assembly complex within the Three-Arms Zone, part of a ₦28.9bn twin project with the State High Court.

December 2025

2026 Appropriation Law

The House passes the ₦617.95bn ‘Budget of Consolidation’ after an upward review of more than ₦82bn.

The Mace

The House cannot sit without the Mace. It is borne in by the Serjeant-at-Arms ahead of the Speaker and rests on the table while the House is in session; when the House resolves into a Committee of the Whole it is placed below the table.
